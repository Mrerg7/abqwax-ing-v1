ABQWax.ing is a spoken-word domain. Read it aloud and it becomes Albuquerque waxing. That is rare: city code, category, and present-tense service inside a single hostname.

## The three parts that make it work

The name breaks into three pieces that each do a job. **ABQ** is the city's own shorthand, the code people already see on the airport, on weather apps, and in local shorthand. **Wax** is the category — the exact word the service is called. **.ing** completes the verb, turning a static label into the action *waxing*.

A domain that contains the query is worth more than a domain that merely hints at it. When someone types "ABQ waxing," the hostname is already the answer.

## Why the .ing TLD matters

`.ing` is not decoration. Compare ABQWax.com and ABQWax.ing. The `.com` reads as a brand label — a company name. The `.ing` reads as a sentence fragment with commercial intent: *ABQ waxing*. It supplies the present-tense verb that turns a noun into a service.

This is the same reason `.ing` works for verbs across categories: booking, shipping, coding, hiring. It converts the name into an action, and in local search, actions are what people look for.

## Scarcity and defensibility

Three-letter city codes that also work in conversation are scarce. ABQ is already the market's shorthand, and pairing it with the waxing category leaves a competitor almost no room to own the same phrase. That is what makes the name defensible — and what gives it value beyond its letters.

## A brand and an asset

The name scales. It works for a single studio, a multi-chair group, or a regional platform. It is short enough for a van wrap, precise enough for paid search and maps, and memorable enough for word of mouth. In every channel, it does the same thing: it says Albuquerque waxing.
