The waxing-versus-shaving debate usually ends the same way once someone tries waxing. The differences come down to how each method treats the hair and the skin.

## What shaving actually does

Shaving cuts hair at the surface of the skin, which is why stubble returns in a day or two. It is fast and inexpensive, but the sharp, blunt tips of cut hair create the familiar rough regrowth, and frequent shaving can lead to razor burn and ingrowns.

## What waxing actually does

Waxing removes hair from the root, so regrowth takes three to four weeks and comes back softer and finer. With consistency, hair density often decreases over time. The trade-off is a brief moment of discomfort during the service and a higher per-session cost — balanced by far less maintenance in between.

## The comparison

- **Frequency:** shaving every day or two; waxing every three to four weeks.
- **Regrowth:** shaving leaves stubble; waxing grows back soft and sparse.
- **Skin:** shaving can cause razor burn and bumps; waxing, done well, leaves skin smooth longer.
- **Cost:** shaving is cheap per session; waxing costs more per session but far less in time and upkeep.

## The takeaway

For areas where smoothness matters and stubble is unwelcome — legs, underarms, and especially Brazilian — waxing wins for most people.
